Customized to the mix, not one map

LiquidJet is a 3D ultra short-loop coldplate whose multi-stage architecture is customized to the GPU power map: the entire flow cools the hotspots first, before the rest of the die. Power Map Studio extends that customization from one power map to the workload mix a fleet actually runs.

An inference fleet spends most of its hours in decode, where the heat moves from the tensor-core GPCs to the HBM PHY and L2. A coldplate staged only to the compute-bound TDP map cools prefill well and leaves decode's hotspots in the last, warmest stage.

    Multi-stage, mapped to the mix

    Each 4.5 mm microcell tile is assigned to stage 1, 2 or 3. The full coolant flow passes stage 1 first, so it gets the coldest coolant and the highest jet velocity. Smaller early stages cool harder but cost pressure drop, so the stage areas are bounded by the psi budget.

    Studio solves a two-layer conduction model of die, TIM and copper base with staged coolant temperatures for every workload, then searches tile assignments (a cut search over a power-weighted tile ranking, followed by seeded boundary moves) to maximize fleet token throughput with DVFS throttling at TJmax = 95 °C.

    With LiquidJet Diamond, Studio also places the diamond inserts where they help the whole mix most.

    How Power Map Studio works

    Thermal model. 48 × 24 cells of 1.5 mm. Die layer (lateral conduction in silicon, weak coupling across die seams and HBM stacks) connected through package + TIM resistance to a copper base plate (diamond inserts add in-plane conductance), which convects to staged coolant. Solved with preconditioned conjugate gradient; the stage coolant temperatures are iterated to energy balance (heat into coolant = GPU power).

    Stages. h ∝ (flow / stage area)0.6 with a geometry ceiling; pressure drop ∝ (flow / stage area)1.75 summed over stages. TJA is the power-weighted die temperature, as defined in Frore's "Thermal Stack" white paper.

    Tokens. At a fixed power cap, leakage doubles every 10 °C and switching energy rises 2% per 10 °C (white-paper rules of thumb, calibrated to −1.5%/°C at 89 °C). If TJmax exceeds 95 °C, DVFS cuts power until it doesn't; that lost throughput is what the optimizer avoids.

    Honest limits. Synthetic floorplan and power maps; correlations are first-order, not CFD; the throttle model is frequency-only DVFS, so it is an upper bound on the loss.
